FCEV passenger-car battery correction

The approved power-scaled Mirai pack proxy is implemented in carculator and all eight FCEV car inventories have been regenerated and installed in Premise. No new runtime calculation is added to Premise. This report isolates this correction against the immediately preceding verified vehicle inventories. The final original-legacy-versus-refreshed comparison remains separate.

Physical calibration

Toyota's manufacturer announcement of 30 November 2020 specifies 310.8 V and 4 Ah (1.2432 kWh), a complete pack mass of 44.6 kg, and 31.5 kW discharge for ten seconds. The model scales nominal capacity and mass together with required buffer power inside its existing vehicle mass/power iteration. This retains the electrical allocation between the fuel cell and battery, including motor losses and auxiliaries. Explicit capacity overrides retain their capacity and must supply the required ten-second buffer power.

Premise representation

All 162 workbook identities are preserved. Substantive coefficient changes affect eight services and eight manufacturing datasets. In each full scenario, 88 activities change: eight RER manufacturing datasets, two unmatched RER services and six operating services in each of thirteen IAM regions. The remaining RER services are unchanged forwarders.

The full-grid float32 calculation takes ten sizing iterations instead of nine. It also shifts 185 non-FCEV workbook exchanges by at most 0.00002322%; these last-place numerical changes are recorded in non_fcev_roundoff.csv. An independent rerun using the preceding pack hook reproduces this effect. No model convergence tolerance was relaxed. There are 700/705 such exchanges in the full 2020/2050 scenarios; no other non-FCEV coefficient changes were found.

All packs retain the MIX capacity market per nominal kWh, including replacements. The physical pack is 35.88 kg/kWh. No mass-equivalence correction is applied to the ordinary capacity market, as explicitly requested. The lower kWh purchase therefore does not establish lower manufacturing burdens for a real high-power pack. Comments and structured provenance disclose this mismatch, the Toyota source, power duration, original model/publication and the retained cell-mass, chemistry and cost proxies. Noise remains in the companion audit file.

All eight FCEV production recipes retain their corrected 2020 battery, motor, inverter and fuel-cell quantities in 2050. Scenario efficiency changes operating fuel demand. Hydrogen and 30-to-700-bar compression electricity remain correctly linked to regional supplies and scale together. Fuel-energy/carbon and capacity allocation checks pass across cars and trucks in both full scenarios.

The largest indicator change and climate change for each class are independently reconciled through an adjoint matrix solve and exchange contributions in incremental_ef31_contributions.csv. Nested manufacturing contributions must not be added to vehicle production twice.

The contribution analysis also checks the largest increase. It identifies:

  • Large EUR carcinogenic toxicity falls 2.55%. The lower MIX battery-capacity purchase accounts for 131% of the net reduction, partly offset by larger fuel-cell/motor components and operating burdens. This is fully explained by the retained capacity-market proxy, but it is not an independently justified manufacturing improvement for the physically heavier pack.

  • Large EUR land use rises 1.22%. The model's mass-dependent road input accounts for 118% of the increase, partly offset by lower battery-capacity burdens.

  • Medium EUR climate impact rises from 0.232464 to 0.232814 kg CO2-eq/km (+0.150%). Battery manufacture falls by 1.876 g CO2-eq/km in the capacity proxy, offsetting additional hydrogen, compression, roads and component manufacture. The small net climate change therefore conceals larger opposing contributions.

No unexplained numerical residual was found. The battery-manufacturing decreases remain a disclosed modelling limitation under the approved MIX market treatment; this calibration does not validate the underlying supply chain or its impact factors.

Limits and reproducibility

This is an approved class proxy, not validation of a complete FCEV design. Ten-second discharge does not establish continuous discharge, charging, SOC, temperature or ageing limits. Charging remains unconstrained and the cycle calculation assumes instantaneous fuel-cell response. No new charging envelope, fuel-cell ramp controller or replacement-life assumption is introduced.
