Small FCEV truck motor calibration

14 October 2026. The user approved documented motor-only proxies of 57 kW for the 3.5 t FCEV and 129 kW for the 7.5 t FCEV. The updated truck workbook is installed in Premise. This step follows the electrical-supply correction.

Diagnosis and scientific boundary

The preceding 2020 Regional-delivery designs had shaft ratings of 51.207 and 93.990 kW, below peak shaft demands of 53.958 and 107.196 kW. The ratings came from generic power-to-curb-mass factors of 25 and 24 W/kg, respectively. Their metadata names car2db, but does not identify a validated FCEV motor. The inherited calculation explicitly uses curb mass; this was not a mistaken conversion of gross mass or fuel-cell electrical input.

An independent reconstruction from speed, acceleration, rolling resistance, aerodynamic drag and gradient reproduces the shared wheel/shaft traces and power deficits. The Regional-delivery peaks occur near 51 km/h while accelerating at about 0.63 m/s2 on 5.2--5.4% grades. Motor/inverter and fuel-cell losses affect upstream energy and electrical supply, not the mechanical motor rating. Electrical auxiliaries bypass the traction motor. carculator_utils already respects these boundaries and preserves unmet driving-cycle energy demand. Its equations required no correction. Four additional regression cases protect this separation.

Approved references

  • Renault, Master Z.E., 1 July 2020 documents a 57 kW motor. The related Master Z.E. Hydrogen was announced for 2020, but this step uses the documented electric-van motor only. It does not copy the hydrogen range extender or claim complete vehicle equivalence.

  • Daimler, FUSO eCanter, 31 March 2020 documents a 129 kW maximum motor rating for the 7.49 t electric truck. Transferring this motor to the 7.5 t FCEV is an explicit powertrain proxy.

Neither reference validates the model's traction-buffer cell capability, fuel-cell dynamics, complete-vehicle mass, or continuous operation at peak motor output. The motor ratings are supported; those other assumptions remain separate.

Implementation and observed changes

carculator_truck accepts a rated power input in kW. A positive value fixes shaft power independently of curb mass. Explicit constructor power overrides take priority; zero or an absent parameter preserves the existing mass-based calculation. The two approved ratings are supplied in the 2020 defaults and retained at the later native milestones, without assuming motor downsizing. Other sizes and powertrains retain their previous defaults.

The existing bounded sizing loop propagates motor, inverter and fuel-cell changes through mass, storage and consumption. The original electrical fuel-cell/battery fractions and energy-sized truck buffers are retained. No additional Premise runtime calculation is introduced.

Long haul is not the selected Premise template duty cycle.

An independent transposed-technosphere solve reproduces each selected LCIA score and closes its direct-exchange contribution balance. Production is then decomposed separately. Fuel-cell stack and balance-of-plant manufacturing explain 91.98% and 88.84% of the acidification increases for 3.5 t and 7.5 t, respectively. The larger net electrical requirement raises stack ratings under the retained 20% fuel-cell / 80% battery allocation. This provides a direct inventory explanation for the largest changes. Increased hydrogen purchases account for only 0.28% and 0.47% of those acidification increases.

For climate change, stack and balance-of-plant manufacturing account for 65.37% and 54.85% of the increases; additional hydrogen contributes 11.27% and 16.52%. The remaining changes include motor/inverter manufacture, road demand and other mass-dependent inputs. No unexplained numerical change was identified in this step. This does not independently validate the inherited fuel-cell allocation or manufacturing inventory. Manufacturing subcomponents explain the vehicle-production contribution and must not be added to that parent contribution a second time.
