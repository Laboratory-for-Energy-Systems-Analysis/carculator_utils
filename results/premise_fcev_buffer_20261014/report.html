FCEV truck battery correction

The approved AKASOL pack proxy is implemented in the native truck model, with shared battery charging constraints in carculator_utils. All seven 2020 FCEV truck inventories have been regenerated and installed in Premise. No new driving-cycle calculation is added to Premise runtime.

This report compares the correction with the immediately preceding verified small-FCEV motor update. It is not the final original-legacy-versus-refreshed vehicle comparison, which remains outstanding.

Physical changes

The AKASOL May 2021 manufacturer sheet is an explicitly approved proxy for 2020: 25 kWh, 238 kg, 36 kW continuous charging, 50 kW continuous RMS discharge, and 150 kW discharge for ten seconds. The same product appears in the earlier manufacturer brochure.

The inherited range-related battery rule is retained as a floor. Packs grow where the cycle's instantaneous battery assistance exceeds the continuous rating. Using that rating at every second avoids inventing a repeated-pulse recovery rule. The full motor's buffer requirement also fits the ten-second rating; indefinite full motor output is not claimed. The 7.5 t and 32 t packs are primarily enlarged by the continuous-discharge requirement.

Regeneration supplies concurrent DC auxiliaries first, then charges up to the continuous battery-terminal limit. Storage losses apply only to the stored portion. Excess braking receives neither fuel nor brake-wear credit. The existing conservative brake-wear conversion discount is retained, including the credit for regeneration used directly by auxiliaries.

The model reconverges vehicle mass, fuel storage and payload. Explicit battery capacities are preserved and rejected if too small at the converged vehicle mass. Propulsion demand is never clipped to hide a power deficit. All seven selected Regional-delivery templates satisfy the motor and battery cycle-power checks; the 57/129 kW small-truck motor proxies remain adequate. Requested cargo is unchanged and within available payload; the target range remains 400 km.

Premise treatment and provenance

The 127-activity workbook preserves every identity. Numerical changes are confined to seven FCEV services and seven production recipes. Other workbook coefficients are unchanged. Both full scenarios differ in only 74 activities: seven RER production recipes, the two unmatched RER services (32 t and 60 t), and five operating services in each of thirteen IAM regions. The other RER services remain forwarders with unchanged forwarding coefficients.

Premise retains the MIX capacity market per nominal kWh including replacements. The physical pack has 9.52 kg/kWh; no mass-equivalence adjustment is applied to the market. Dataset comments and structured metadata disclose this mismatch, the reference edition, conditioning assumption, power boundaries and retained manufacturing proxies. Model name, publication, source hashes and original component provenance are retained. Saved Brightway metadata matches exactly.

The seven FCEV production recipes keep the regenerated 2020 battery, motor, inverter and fuel-cell quantities in 2050. Scenario efficiency still changes operating energy. Future FCEV hardware resizing remains outside the approved scope. Hydrogen and its 30-to-700-bar compression electricity remain linked and scaled consistently through the regional scenario supplies.

All paired changes are positive, from 0.17% to 6.94%. Exchange contribution analysis independently reconciles the largest indicator and climate change for each truck against the forward LCIA solve. The dominant explanations are:

  • 3.5 t land use: increased road input accounts for 98.6% of the increase.

  • 7.5 t carcinogenic toxicity: added battery-capacity manufacture accounts for 87.7% of the increase.

  • 32 t non-carcinogenic toxicity: battery-capacity manufacture explains 50.5% and brake-wear treatment 16.5% of the increase.

  • 18 t, 26 t and 40 t ozone depletion: hydrogen supply explains 69.1%, 82.6% and 86.3% of their respective increases.

  • 60 t ionising radiation: compression electricity and hydrogen supply explain 50.9% and 45.7% of the increase.

Manufacturing sub-contributions are nested within vehicle production and must not be added to it a second time. No unexplained numerical residual or change outside the FCEV correction was found. This explains the changes within the chosen inventory and LCIA assumptions; it is not independent validation of the background supply-chain impact factors.

Limits and retained assumptions

This is a conditioned-pack engineering proxy, not certification at arbitrary SOC or temperature. No SOC controller, fuel-cell ramp, thermal model or revised ageing calculation is introduced. The existing cycle-average fuel conversion and battery replacement assumptions are retained. The 70% internal cell mass allocation and NMC-622 inventory/cost mapping are proxies; the effective cell density reproduces the documented whole-pack mass rather than claiming a measured AKASOL cell specification. Other chemistry choices retain their inventory identity while using the supplied physical pack profile.

Future native-model years retain this pack profile unless explicitly overridden. The approved Premise scenarios instead retain the complete 2020 FCEV hardware. Cars, buses and two-wheelers do not opt into the new charging constraint by default. The separately skipped PHEV cycling review remains skipped.
