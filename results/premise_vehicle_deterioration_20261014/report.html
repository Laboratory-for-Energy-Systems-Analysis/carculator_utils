Hybrid hot-exhaust deterioration correction

14 October 2026. This update follows the FCEV accounting correction and isolates hybrid pollutant ageing. It is not the final old-versus-new vehicle EF 3.1 comparison.

Finding and correction

Hot-exhaust coefficients already map HEVs and PHEV combustion modes to their petrol or diesel ICEV engine. The mileage-deterioration lookup instead selected literal hybrid labels. Truck/bus tables have no hybrid rows; the car table contains unity placeholders but no corresponding Euro-6 hybrid endpoint data. Both paths consequently gave hybrids unity rather than the engine factor.

The lookup now uses the same engine aliases as hot-factor selection, then restores the original powertrain labels before multiplication. Repeated aliases do not collapse HEV/PHEV coordinates. Missing source factors retain unity, and the absence of a two-wheeler table still means no correction.

No numerical coefficients or mileage assumptions change. For endpoint factor f, vehicle lifetime L and reference mileage K, the existing lifetime-average multiplier remains 1 + (max(f, 1) - 1) * L / (2*K). K is 200,000 km for cars and 890,000 km for trucks/buses. The formula represents linear deterioration from unity, averaged over vehicle lifetime. It retains linear extrapolation beyond the reference mileage.

This is a combustion-engine ageing proxy for hybrids. It is not a measured hybrid-specific deterioration curve. The existing lifetime basis is vehicle kilometres, with no new engine-on-distance or PHEV utility-factor adjustment. PHEV combustion-mode emissions are weighted once when the final vehicle is assembled. Cold-start and evaporative factors are added after hot-exhaust deterioration and do not acquire this multiplier. Dataset comments disclose these boundaries.

Petrol aggregate hydrocarbon diagnostics also acquire a 1.01 multiplier. This aggregate is not exported as an additional biosphere flow; NMHC species, PAH and formaldehyde inventories are unchanged. Total car CO/NOx increases are smaller than the hot multipliers because cold starts are unchanged. For the Medium petrol HEV, CO rises from 0.60706 to 0.64976 g/km (+7.03%) and NOx from 0.03542 to 0.03875 g/km (+9.41%).

Truck NOx multipliers range from 1.24449 to 1.63820 with the existing size- specific lifetimes. The 40 t diesel HEV rises from 1.71022 to 2.80169 g NOx/km (+63.82%). The 40 t PHEV combustion mode rises from 1.67689 to 2.74708 g/km; its final service retains the existing combustion-driving share. Approved gasoline hybrid truck proxies inherit the corresponding diesel-engine correction. Truck CO and all fuel inputs remain unchanged.

The large truck NOx increase is explained by the previously bypassed ageing multiplier. It does not establish the validity of the underlying high hot-emission coefficient. The original truck g/MJ derivation is unavailable, as confirmed by the user. No guessed coefficient replacement, clipping or compensating fuel-efficiency adjustment is applied.

Fresh generation changes only CO/NOx in 32 car services and NOx in 16 truck services. All production recipes, other biosphere flows, fuel/electricity inputs, battery specifications and vehicle identities are preserved. The 162-car and 127-truck activity catalogues remain unchanged. Bus model calculations inherit the shared lookup correction; the Premise bus and two-wheeler workbooks are not refreshed in this step.
