Vehicle energy efficiency and direct-emission drivers

Status: emission-scaling correction verified; constant-range hardware sizing remains in progress. This is the first independent part of remediation step 8.

Finding and correction

Premise derives its vehicle efficiency index from final energy divided by reported transport service, relative to 2020. Follow-up inspection of the actual encrypted scenario corrected the preliminary unit interpretation: car service is passenger-kilometres, despite the mapping comment saying vehicle-kilometres; truck service is tonne-kilometres. Premise applies the car intensity ratio to fixed-occupancy vehicle-kilometre inventories. The default upper index bound of 1.25 limits the corresponding energy reduction to 20%. This is an overall service energy intensity, not an isolated motor/engine efficiency. Missing alias pairs and zero-baseline backfilling need separate treatment before physical future sizing; see the range audit. Future native carculator improvements must not be applied in addition to the same IAM reduction.

The old transport transformation multiplied every biosphere exchange by the energy-demand factor. The combustion-car energy floor additionally rescaled several HBEFA pollutants. Neither operation establishes a physical relationship between energy demand and distance/start-based emission factors.

The correction uses the model's existing inventory equations:

  • Burned-fuel CO2 follows fuel purchases; the fuel module still resolves the final regional fossil/non-fossil split.

  • Sulphur dioxide follows fuel mass at the existing sulphur concentration.

  • Generic-air methane leakage follows fuel throughput. Urban/rural/highway HBEFA combustion methane is separate and retains its distance basis.

  • HBEFA hot, cold-start and evaporative emissions are not rescaled by this energy-intensity operation. This includes NOx, PM, CO, NMVOC, formaldehyde, benzene and PAHs. No new emission factors or speciation are introduced.

  • Refrigerants and other distance/time/resource flows are not energy-scaled. Wear treatment and manufacturing technosphere exchanges already lay outside the energy selection and remain unchanged in this commit.

Premise's separate GAINS pollutant projections remain active. Therefore this correction does not imply identical 2020 and 2050 pollutant inventories. Neither the plausibility screen nor the correction clips emissions to a EURO limit. Buses/two-wheelers and other transport modules retain their existing behavior pending their own inventory updates.

Validation

Both complete builds use REMIND SSP2-NPi, ecoinvent 3.12 cutoff, identical encrypted IAM input and workbooks, and an isolated Brightway project copy. No persistent user database was written. The control is the completed truck HEV correction in premise_truck_hev_storage_20261013.

Every material change equals removal of the previous unsupported scaling, including the old energy-floor correction where applicable. Changes below 1e-9 relative or 1e-13 absolute are counted separately as roundoff. Suppliers, reference identities, fuel-derived CO2 and all technosphere coefficients are unchanged within that tolerance.

The 47 additional warnings all belong to the inherited hot-only 0.5–2 times screen. That screen uses a fuel-normalized reference; its reference declines with energy demand while corrected distance/start-based emissions do not. These are screening disagreements, not newly demonstrated EURO-limit failures. Warnings remain visible and are not suppressed to obtain a clean report.

The EUR 40 t BEV's R134a emission increases from 1.0283e-7 to 1.2854e-7 kg/tkm: removing the old 0.8 multiplier raises the value 25%. The EUR Mini gasoline car's urban formaldehyde changes from 8.5224e-7 to 8.7526e-7 kg/km, about 2.70%. Across affected exchanges, changes range from +2.09% to +25%. These increases remove an unsupported future benefit; they do not revise HBEFA factors.

All 121 focused tests pass, including 28 emission-driver regressions. They exercise conventional and electrified cars, trucks, compact exchange records, uncertainty scaling, a binding energy floor, methane compartments, unrelated burdens, invalid factors, regional fuel-carbon accounting, hydrogen compression and physical battery purchases. Runtime source changes are confined to transport.py, car_energy.py and vehicle_emissions.py.

Remaining hardware work

Battery quantities and vehicle hardware intentionally have not yet changed in this independent correction. The user approved the original chemistry's Premise density projection for BEV/PHEV physical mass, including NMC-622 0.24 to 0.26 kWh/kg from 2020 to 2050. High-power HEV specifications remain separate. The approved range policy still requires consistent capacity, mass, power, lifetime and inventory propagation without double-counting IAM gains.
