Truck HEV traction batteries

13 October 2026. Remediation-plan step 7.

Correction and scope

The seven native hybrid trucks previously had zero traction-battery capacity, mass, manufacture and disposal, while receiving regenerative fuel savings. Their small power-battery mass represented the lead-acid starter. The missing traction pack is now sized jointly with vehicle mass, motor power and fuel consumption. Only the nine HEV service templates (seven diesel hybrids and two documented gasoline proxies) and seven production recipes change materially. All 127 workbook identities are retained. Other vehicle powertrains remain unchanged within numerical tolerance.

The user approved power/energy sizing with the existing motor design, retaining Premise's MIX capacity market despite its physical mass mismatch, and using existing chemistry-specific prices as explicit financial proxies. Native manufacture and disposal use physical pack mass. The adapted Premise workbook instead purchases nominal capacity times one plus the replacement count, with no additional pack-mass or disposal exchange.

The model now separates the lead-acid starter from the traction pack, includes its mass in the truck and supplies at least one starter, retaining the existing five-year starter lifetime. The older fixed 16 kg inventory recipe is unchanged for other powertrains.

Sizing and scientific boundary

The power-oriented sizing follows the distinction made in Vijayagopal and Rousseau (2020), Benefits of Electrified Powertrains in Medium- and Heavy-Duty Vehicles. Their 5--8 kWh packs are a plausibility reference, not a universal capacity: the present model's motor ratings differ. Motor-design parameters are retained; absolute kW change slightly as battery mass enters the existing sizing loop.

The reference is the pre-2020 A123 Systems AHR32113M1Ultra-B high-power LFP cell: 3.3 V, 4.5 Ah, 205 g. See the manufacturer's 2014 Battery Pack Design, Validation, and Assembly Guide, document 493005-002 Rev 02, Tables 4 and 30--33. Continuous limits at 25 degrees C are 33 A charging and 200 A discharging. Multiplication by the 2 V cutoff gives conservative 66 W and 400 W limits per cell. Pulse ratings are not used continuously. A 60% usable SOC window and 70% cell mass share are explicit engineering assumptions.

The pack supports the full motor discharge rating and the cycle's required usable energy. A finite, periodic buffer accepts braking energy up to the charge-power and capacity limits and reuses it during subsequent propulsion. Excess energy gets neither a fuel-saving credit nor a brake-wear reduction. There is no external electricity or engine charging and no free initial energy. Fuel conversion retains the model's existing cycle-average conversion boundary. The positive-propulsion fuel limit is applied after the actual battery reuse is known. This order is tested independently for a surplus-braking downhill cycle; scaling an already capped unrestricted credit would understate the fuel displaced by the accepted energy.

An initial proposal to accept every braking peak continuously produced about 52 kWh and one tonne for the 40 t truck. That design was rejected before final installation. The final model permits curtailed regenerative braking, as a finite HEV buffer should. The first private scenario build also preceded the brake-wear correction; it is archived separately and is not final evidence.

Explicit limitations

  • Cell ratings assume a conditioned pack at 25 degrees C. Thermal conditioning demand, low-temperature derating, resistance ageing, pack electrical layout and an optimized hybrid controller are not independently modelled.

  • Generic chemistry cycle life and prices are retained proxies. There is no separate HEV calendar-age model; replacement predictions have this boundary.

  • The user explicitly retained the MIX capacity market. Its chemistry and mass do not match the physical high-power LFP pack. For comparison, the ordinary 2020 LFP recipe supplies 8.6 kg/kWh, versus this pack's 19.7 kg/kWh; MIX mass varies with chemistry shares. Manufacture and disposal remain internally consistent within the capacity market, but do not represent the physical vehicle mass. No hidden mass-equivalence correction was applied.

  • Native future-year model runs and Premise 2050 transformations are distinct. This step verifies integration; it does not implement the separately planned range-preserving future hardware resizing. The PHEV regenerative-cycling decision also remains open and was not inferred from the HEV authorization. The nine HEV services remain RER templates without a separate mapped IAM efficiency signal. Their fuel quantities remain at the corrected 2020 level in the 2050 build, while their fuel suppliers are EUR scenario markets and fossil/non-fossil CO2 follows those suppliers. This is not evidence of a newly sized 2050 HEV fleet.

Verification evidence

The permanent regressions cover independent power/energy balances, periodic storage, braking curtailment, invalid ratings, chemistry/capacity/cost overrides, named samples, repeated runs, input preservation, native manufacture/disposal, starter separation, equivalent-full-cycle accounting and Premise capacity purchases and metadata. A historical resource audit still verifies all original price values and uncertainty bounds after undoing the documented HEV scope extension.

The full REMIND SSP2-NPi builds contain 42,564 activities in 2020 and 42,574 in 2050, with zero validation errors and the same 401/398 warning identities as before. In each build, 589 materially changed exchanges are confined to the 16 HEV service/production activities. Other differences are below 2.9e-13 in absolute exchange amount. Each build passes 495 fuel-carbon and 147 hydrogen-compression checks; the seven new battery-capacity purchases agree with nominal packs and replacement counts. Seventy focused Premise regressions pass.

All 1768 tests pass against the final installed family wheels (1377 shared, 77 car, 171 truck, 43 bus and 100 two-wheeler), with no failures, errors or skips. This includes 20 new HEV cases. Wheel and sdist-rebuilt wheel resources match, all five changed truck runtime files match the source bytes, and the offline core-only model/LCIA smoke checks pass for both artifact routes.

Final scenario checks and installed-artifact results are recorded in verification_summary.json and installed_artifact_verification.json. Full databases and validation details remain in the private build workspace.
