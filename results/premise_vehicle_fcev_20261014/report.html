FCEV buffer power and pack accounting

14 October 2026. This isolates the FCEV accounting correction from the preceding hybrid petrol-mapping correction. It is not the final old-versus-new vehicle EF 3.1 comparison requested for the end of the exercise.

Correction and scope

The source parameter defines fuel-cell share as the fraction of total vehicle power supplied by the fuel cell. The model's stack rating includes its own consumption. The former battery formula multiplied that gross rating by the complementary share, applying the share twice and leaving a net-power gap. The corrected nominal allocation is:

gross stack kW = vehicle kW * fuel-cell share * gross/net multiplier
buffer kW = vehicle kW * (1 - fuel-cell share)
gross stack kW / gross/net multiplier + buffer kW = vehicle kW

For the power-sized car buffer, cell mass is buffer power divided by cell specific power. The declared cell fraction describes cell mass divided by complete pack mass. Pack mass is therefore cell mass divided by that fraction, with balance-of-pack mass equal to the difference. Multiplying cell mass by one minus its fraction previously understated the balance of pack.

The starter-battery helper no longer overwrites FCEV traction power with zero. Combustion starter sizing retains its existing convention. The truck HEV's separate traction buffer and starter are preserved. The shared fix also applies to bus model calculations; the Premise bus workbook is not refreshed here.

Interpretation limits

The truck and bus energy-sizing rule remains 20 kWh plus 6% of hydrogen chemical energy. No new cell technology or buffer-sizing rule is introduced. The truck's inherited 0.15 kW/kg input describes lead-acid starters; it is not a validated lithium-ion traction rating and does not determine the final energy-sized FCEV pack. Correcting the recorded power does not verify peak cell discharge capability, independent motor/inverter peak losses, or sustained cycle power delivery. These limitations appear in the truck dataset comments. Default truck FCEV buffer price inputs remain zero; no unapproved price proxy has been introduced. A regression confirms that a supplied price uses the corrected nonzero power requirement. Costs do not enter these LCA exchanges.

Future FCEV buffers and hydrogen tanks retain the baseline design under the existing policy. The correction introduces no additional Premise runtime calculation. No hot-emission factors, fuel-blend assumptions, compression pressure, PHEV packs or HEV design assumptions are changed.
