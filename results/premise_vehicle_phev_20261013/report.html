PHEV installed battery capacity correction

13 October 2026. Remediation-plan step 5; replacement-count accounting is next.

Outcome

The shared PHEV aggregation now retains the full electric-mode nominal battery capacity and cell mass share along with the already retained pack, cell and balance-of-pack masses. Electric driving share still weights operation. Electric range now uses nominal capacity times depth of discharge rather than nominal capacity alone. No emission factors, driving shares, physical masses, fuel consumption, electricity consumption or battery replacement counts were changed.

All 16 PHEV car production templates now purchase their full nominal capacity. Purchases increase 19.0--36.1% relative to the previously understated quantity (the previous deficit was 16.0--26.5% of the physical pack). For the Medium car, 127.4 kg of cells at 0.24 kWh/kg gives 30.576 kWh, replacing 23.894781 kWh. Both gasoline and diesel variants use this same capacity. No hard-coded capacity was substituted: the model retains its sized electric-mode hardware.

The truck adapter previously recovered capacity from full pack mass. It now uses the corrected model field, with independent mass/density checks, as the car adapter does. Truck purchases are numerically unchanged within float32 rounding. For the 40 t PHEV, the model now reports 77.323265 kWh rather than its usage-weighted 11.598490 kWh. The existing 0.323333 replacement count still yields 102.324451 lifetime kWh purchased; its scientific interpretation remains step 6. The two mode ranges remain 60 km electric and 400 km combustion; their combined full-charge/full-tank range is now 460 km, rather than 411.25 km.

The 162 car and 127 truck dataset identities, including names, products, units and geographies, are preserved. Capacity markets include manufacture and end of life, so the adapters continue to remove both mass-based pack purchases and separate pack disposal. Noise stays in the companion files.

Cost consistency

An existing battery-price regression failed after the initial capacity fix: purchase costs still priced the smaller, usage-weighted pack. Cars and trucks now price the full electric-mode nominal capacity in both intermediate cost calculations. The existing subclass formulas then propagate that purchase cost through financing and, for trucks, insurance and residual credit. Operating energy and charging costs retain their usage weighting. Mode-specific unit prices, markup and explicit cost overrides keep their existing conventions.

This change does not respecify other PHEV components or reconcile all differences between mode-specific vehicle masses. Coherent future resizing remains step 8.

Verification

Six new shared-model regression cases exercise car and truck PHEVs at electric shares 0, 0.5 and 1, with reordered years and two labelled samples. They verify physical capacity, cell/pack mass balance, usable range, battery price and unchanged usage weighting of operation. All six failed before the correction. Four new Premise checks inspect every installed PHEV template and its physical capacity, lifetime purchase and absence of duplicate disposal.

The final focused test commands passed:

  • Shared capacity, battery costs, charging costs, repeated runs and cost-label alignment: 102 tests.

  • Car inputs/components: 13 tests; car battery costs: 3 tests.

  • Truck inputs/components/charger inventory/depot throughput: 54 tests; cost defaults, infrastructure finance and AdBlue: 43 tests.

  • Premise battery-capacity, hydrogen, fuel-carbon and car integration: 49 tests.

These six runs total 264 passing tests. Premise reported two existing dependency warnings. Earlier shared inventory/export/LCIA and base-contract checks also passed; their run exposed the cost regression described above, subsequently fixed and rerun. Real inventory checks covered the bus and two-wheeler siblings, whose models do not call the PHEV aggregation method.

Fresh car/truck exports from the final cost-corrected models exactly reproduce the physical parameters, noise, comments and inventory coefficients installed before the cost correction. The reproduction record captures final source hashes. Exact ecoinvent 3.12 supplier checks find no missing links in either catalogue.

Full builds use REMIND SSP2-NPi and ecoinvent 3.12 cutoff, in isolated temporary Brightway storage. The persistent user databases were not written.

Reproduction and records

dev/regenerate_premise_cars.py and dev/regenerate_premise_trucks.py were run without --reuse-raw under Python 3.12.13 / NumPy 1.26.4 with matching sibling source checkouts. Both reject an inconsistent PHEV physical pack. The guarded installer saves private rollback copies and verifies both families before replacing either workbook. The build and audit scripts are dev/build_premise_vehicle_phev.py and dev/audit_premise_vehicle_phev.py.

Companion records:

  • workbook_installation.json: input/output hashes and private rollback paths.

  • car_battery_capacity_changes.csv and scenario_battery_capacity_changes.csv: all changed capacity purchases.

  • build_2020.json and build_2050.json: build settings, source/IAM hashes, installed workbook hashes, validation counts and private inventory locations.

  • verification_summary.json: scenario audit counts and numerical bounds.

  • final_model_reproduction.json: fresh final-model replay and source hashes.
