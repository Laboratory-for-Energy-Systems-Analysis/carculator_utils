BEV truck payload at fixed capacity utilization

The lighter future battery now increases carried cargo at unchanged 2020 payload utilization. This implements the user's requested weight-capacity policy for BEV trucks. Gross mass, driver mass, non-battery hardware and lifetime kilometres retain their reference values. Cars and PHEV trucks retain their actual loads.

Available payload equals gross mass minus curb mass minus driver mass. The baseline cargo / available-payload ratio is applied to the new available payload. Every non-production exchange in the transport service is multiplied by baseline cargo / new cargo. Truck manufacture per tkm therefore remains 1 / (lifetime vehicle-km times cargo tonnes). The service output is unchanged; manufacturing one truck is not itself divided by cargo.

Electricity per tonne-kilometre

Electricity per tkm equals electricity per vehicle-km divided by cargo tonnes. The IAM-derived electricity per vehicle-km is retained; carrying more cargo divides this same energy by more tonne-kilometres. Battery capacity, chemistry, physical pack mass and replacement count are unchanged from the preceding constant-range implementation. No additional load-dependent driving-energy calculation is introduced. In particular, the lower per-tkm electricity is not fed back into battery sizing on a repeated transformation.

This is a transparent accounting approximation: the IAM trend is interpreted at the original load, with this explicit fixed-utilization benefit added. It does not establish that every IAM independently projects that payload gain, nor simulate cargo-volume constraints or new power/thermal requirements. Road construction and wear do use the new loaded driving mass, including the extra cargo, before their per-tkm normalization. The dataset comment and premise vehicle battery sizing fields record the new cargo and utilization.

Verification

276 focused tests pass, including 53 battery-sizing tests. New cases cover partial/full utilization, smaller/larger packs, gross-mass feasibility, per-tkm purchases, uncertainty scaling, compact exchanges, non-unit service output, loaded wear, range conservation, exact repeatability and unchanged 2020.

Complete encrypted-IAM builds cover 2020, 2030 and 2050. Each checks all 65 regional BEV truck services. Independent checks reconstruct payload, mass, per-vehicle-km electricity, range and lifetime truck purchases. Comparing every inventory exchange against the preceding constant-load builds explains all differences: zero changes in 2020 and 585 changes in each future build. All per-vehicle manufacturing inventories and other vehicle service inventories retain their preceding exchange quantities. There are zero validation errors; warning identities/counts are unchanged (401, 427 and 460 respectively).

The complete car/truck battery and payload step takes 0.042, 0.054 and 0.053 seconds in the three builds. Total builds take approximately 100--105 seconds. No carculator runtime, driving-cycle simulation or iterative solver is added.

Reproducibility

build_2020.json, build_2030.json and build_2050.json record the unchanged workbook/encrypted-IAM hashes, current source hashes, private inventory hashes and timings. verification_summary.json records the checks and test counts. verification_sources.json identifies the audit/test sources. vehicle_designs.csv contains all 195 year/region/design rows and inventory_changes.csv all 1,170 changed coefficients. Full inventories and validation reports stay local; no persistent user Brightway database was written.

Build command, repeated for each year:

/private/tmp/premise-metals-venv/bin/python dev/build_premise_vehicle_battery.py \
  --year 2050 --workspace /private/tmp/premise-vehicle-payload-20261013 \
  --output results/premise_vehicle_payload_20261013
/private/tmp/premise-metals-venv/bin/python dev/audit_premise_vehicle_payload.py

The preceding comparison is ../premise_vehicle_battery_simple_20261013; the build script also retains the earlier IAM-restoration provenance checks. The existing baseline workbooks needed no changes for this payload policy.
