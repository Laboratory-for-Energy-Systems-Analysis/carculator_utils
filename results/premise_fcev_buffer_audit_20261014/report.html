FCEV truck battery power-envelope audit

Status: source review and current-template screening complete; reference-pack adoption awaits the user's scientific-assumption decision. No model default, inventory workbook or Premise database has been changed by this audit.

Finding

The current FCEV truck calculation has no traction-pack charge-power limit. The shared energy model limits regenerative mechanical power by motor rating, then applies generator and battery efficiencies. The truck sizes its final battery from the inherited 20 kWh + 6% of stored hydrogen energy rule. Neither step establishes that the battery can accept the resulting power.

The inherited 0.15 kW/kg input is documented as lead-acid starter-battery specific power. It is not evidence for the lithium-ion traction pack. Changing only that input would not resolve the issue: truck storage sizing overwrites the temporary power-sized FCEV battery from the parent method.

A shared correction is appropriate for optional battery charging constraints and their energy accounting. Truck-specific reference specifications and final capacity/mass sizing belong in carculator_truck. Premise would import the recalculated baseline and metadata; it needs no new driving-cycle solver.

Reference and its limitations

The candidate reference is the AKASOL AKASYSTEM 15 OEM 37 PRC. The earlier manufacturer brochure documents the product by 2020, at 24.5 kWh and about 238 kg. It gives configuration-dependent continuous and peak power ranges but no separate charging limits.

The May 2021 manufacturer sheet provides a coherent pack specification: 25 kWh, 238 kg including the contactor box, 50 kW continuous RMS discharge, 150 kW discharge for 10 seconds, 36 kW continuous charging, and 86 kW charging for 10 seconds. Ratings depend on SOC, temperature and integration. Applying this edition to 2020 requires an explicit proxy decision. The sheet does not establish NMC-622 stoichiometry, internal cell mass fraction or the recovery interval for repeated pulses.

The audit scales this complete reference by nominal kWh, without claiming an integer-module vehicle design. It does not combine its power limits with the current 168 Wh/kg pack mass. At unchanged capacity, its 105 Wh/kg pack basis would imply 677 kg for the 40 t vehicle, compared with the current 423 kg. This is an alternative design, not proof that all lighter packs are invalid.

Sensitivity results

At the existing nominal capacities, the 26 t, 40 t and 60 t net charging peaks exceed even this reference's 10-second charging ceiling. Every truck's rated buffer requirement and cycle discharge peak is below its scaled 10-second discharge ceiling. The 7.5 t and 32 t traces exceed the continuous RMS rating for up to 6 and 18 consecutive seconds respectively. A full-cycle RMS value cannot certify their pulse/recovery behaviour: the datasheet does not define an averaging interval. No automatic discharge-capability pass is assigned.

A conservative sensitivity clips regeneration at the continuous charging limit, after allowing direct use by concurrent DC loads. It holds vehicle mass, stack, capacity and the existing round-trip energy convention fixed. The resulting increase in calculated hydrogen energy use is:

These are screening sensitivities, not corrected vehicle predictions or LCIA results. A production change must also converge mass, fuel storage, payload and sizing, preserve the requested speed trace, and check power again at the new mass. In particular, heavier small trucks must still meet the cycle with their recently approved fixed motor ratings.

The existing optimistic usable-energy bound remains only a lower bound. It has no FC ramp dynamics or finite FC-to-battery charge power. The FCEV defaults specify no usable SOC window, so nominal capacity alone cannot certify charge-sustaining operation. The present audit does not reopen the separately skipped PHEV replacement/cycling review.

Implementation and regression scope after the proxy decision

  • In utils, impose the selected pack charge limit at the DC boundary, before storage losses. Account for concurrent auxiliary use and dissipated braking explicitly. Preserve the requested propulsion demand; report a discharge shortfall rather than clipping away its energy.

  • In trucks, keep power requirements separate from battery capabilities and apply a coherent pack energy/mass/power profile. Respect explicit capacity overrides, converge mass/storage/load, and check sustained demand without treating a 10-second rating as indefinitely available.

  • Update regenerative fuel credits and brake-wear credits consistently. Cover zero regeneration, binding/nonbinding charge limits, mixed powertrains, conversion losses, invalid active-vehicle inputs, and native truck cycles with independent energy-balance regressions. Inspect car/bus inheritance before enabling any shared hook.

  • Retain Premise's approved MIX capacity-market treatment, reporting any physical mass mismatch. Rebuild 2020/2050, verify fuel/carbon and capacity links, and rerun the incremental EF comparison if the vehicles change.
