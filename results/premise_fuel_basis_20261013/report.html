Vehicle fuel energy and carbon properties: step 2

Completed on 13 October 2026. The refreshed 2020 car/truck templates now use 46.5 MJ/kg delivered CNG, as approved by the user and documented by the source ecoinvent vehicle-grade gas market. Eight car and seven truck service inventories buy 2.15054% more CNG for unchanged model energy. Exact dataset identities, hardware, non-CNG exchanges, model parameters and noise are unchanged. A parsed-workbook comparison verified every field; only those 15 fuel amounts and their exchange comments differ. Workbook provenance records the correction and identifies the frozen raw exports used to isolate it.

The existing compression recipe is preserved. It purchases about 1.360544 m3 of gas and 0.361 kWh per kg. At Premise's 36 MJ/m3 reference, the gas input is 48.97959 MJ/kg. This is an upstream boundary, not delivered heating value. The 2.47959 MJ/kg difference from 46.5 is explicitly recorded, not assigned entirely to measured methane losses. The earlier inference of 48.98 MJ/kg as delivered fuel was superseded by the source documentation and the user's choice.

Corrected market construction

The documentation promised energy-equivalent fuel markets, but the builder normalized supplier quantities after conversion. Liquid-fuel callers did not apply their differing LHVs; the gas caller also summed kg and m3 quantities. The correction normalizes eligible IAM energy shares first, then purchases energy_share * reference_LHV / supplier_LHV. Other sector market builders retain their original default behavior through an explicit fuel-only option.

Petrol, diesel, kerosene, gas, hydrogen and scenario LPG markets now use this energy-aware construction. Natural-gas markets use 36 MJ/m3; kg methane inputs use 47.5 MJ/kg and the energy conversion 36/47.5, not a density conversion. Electricity, transport, infrastructure and other ancillary market inputs remain additional burdens. World markets aggregate reference-energy regional outputs. Gas markets receive the explicit IAM blend, including marginal mixes where applicable, while absolute production volumes still determine World weights.

Carbon provenance and limits

Structured premise fuel properties metadata record units, supplier quantities, delivered/reference energy, fossil/non-fossil oxidation potential, unresolved carbon and source evidence. The CNG entries are calculated after final fuel relinking, including all regional compression/market chains and the original forwarding datasets. Captured-carbon credits and other carbon pollutants are unchanged: this is a full-oxidation accounting convention, not an elemental carbon closure.

Synthetic routes are traced through carbon-bearing intermediates, excluding hydrogen, electricity and plant infrastructure. DAC carbon is atmospheric and uses the non-fossil combustion category; that does not mean it is biomass. Cement and municipal-waste CO2 retain the source inventories' explicit non-fossil fractions of 6% and 61.3636%. These are inherited source assumptions, not newly derived regional or future capture compositions. Coal-derived carbon is fossil even when the supplier name emphasizes its hydrogen route. The erroneous fossil-origin flags for coal methane and natural-gas-derived kerosene were corrected. Unknown carbon remains explicit and produces a diagnostic; none remained in the two tested scenario builds.
