Hydrogen tank compression: step 4

Completed on 13 October 2026. Hydrogen vehicle compression electricity now follows final hydrogen consumption. The user approved the documented Premise delivery pressure of 30 bar, replacing the generic 25-bar assumption. The same compressor model requires 2.450619 kWh/kg H2 to reach 700 bar, instead of 2.616238. All 147 marked hydrogen car/truck services pass the independent check in each completed 2020 and 2050 scenario.

The existing fuel module was investigated first

HydrogenMixin creates the regional low-pressure hydrogen market and adds one kilogram of hydrogen supply, distributed by pipeline service per kilogram supplied. The source inventory describes 250 km of transmission, regional storage and another 250 km to the consumer, arriving at 30 bar. Its direct electricity requirement is 1.816554 kWh/kg; production, infrastructure, storage and loss replacement have their own upstream burdens.

This is already part of fuel supply. It does not include compression from the delivered pressure to the vehicle's 700-bar tank. The older add_compression_electricity function, which mentions 900-bar dispensing, has no call site in the current production/market pipeline. Its presence in the source was therefore not evidence that tank preparation was counted twice.

The audit follows every marked vehicle's actual hydrogen market, including original-geography and World forwarders, to the delivery inventories. Twelve distinct regional delivery chains are reached in each year; each retains the documented 30-bar boundary. Original RER, CN and US links are retained where they serve the corresponding regional chain. The upstream recipes are unchanged.

The confirmed scaling defect was downstream: truck fuel aliases selected hydrogen but omitted the separate tank-compression electricity exchange. Hydrogen purchases fell with IAM efficiency, while that electricity stayed fixed. A blanket electricity alias would also affect unrelated power and could double-scale explicitly handled compression.

Correction and boundaries

The eight car and seven truck FCEV templates retain their hydrogen demand, hardware, tank pressure, exact identities and all non-compression exchanges. Only tank-compression electricity and its identifying metadata are adapted. The compressor assumptions remain the carculator_utils defaults: 300 K, three equal-pressure-ratio intercooled stages, ideal hydrogen with gamma 1.4, 0.56 isentropic efficiency and 0.92 motor efficiency. The pressure correction reduces specific electricity by approximately 6.33%. It is a supply-boundary adaptation, not an assumed compressor-efficiency improvement over time. Precooling and fast-fill overpressure remain outside this model calculation.

The hydrogen tank compression activity metadata identify the fuel product, electricity product, pressures, specific requirement, source and boundary. They survive Excel import and regional relinking, which can aggregate exchanges and discard their individual comments. The transport helper separates the identified compression share before generic efficiency scaling, then sets it once from final H2 purchases. A residual for other uses of the same electricity product is retained, including when relinking merges them into one exchange.

premise hydrogen compression balance records final H2, compression energy and the other-electricity remainder. Validation checks these against current exchanges. Retained templates receive a record; forwarding originals do not retain a stale direct-use record. Unmarked legacy inventories are not assigned an inferred compression purpose. Future old/new inventory comparisons must make any compatibility adaptation explicit.

The regeneration adapters now calculate compression from the final purchased H2 quantity after the reference-fuel conversion. This avoids small differences from float32 source-export rounding. The installation receipt checks original workbook hashes, preserves baseline copies privately, and verifies parsed inventories against the intended changes. Truck parameter sidecars update their seven compression values; car model parameters and both noise files remain unchanged. Workbook provenance records the approved adaptation.

Replaying both adapters from the frozen raw model exports independently reproduces all 5,451 car and 3,556 truck numerical coefficients across the installed 162 and 127 datasets, respectively (relative tolerance 1e-12, absolute tolerance 1e-18). The replay does not rerun or change vehicle sizing. adapter_reproduction.json records the checks and adapter source hashes.
