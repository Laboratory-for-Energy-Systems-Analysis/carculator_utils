Exact IAM-variable and unit audit

The checked input is the same encrypted REMIND SSP2-NPi file used in both controlled scenario builds, SHA256 beginning 3a9f77641a1cf287. These findings concern that file and the current mappings, not every REMIND release.

  • Of 35 car mapping entries, 30 declare REMIND service aliases. Only 10 have both their exact service and energy aliases in the file. The other five entries have no declared REMIND mapping and are not unexpected missing data.

  • The existing mappings request FE variables ending in BEV, FCEV and Hybrid electric that are absent. Carrier-level Electricity and Hydrogen labels are present, but their existence alone does not establish a matching technology-specific numerator, especially for BEV/PHEV electricity.

  • The requested Large Car class is absent: the file uses Large Car and SUV. Sharing this signal between templates or splitting fleet volumes needs an explicit rule; duplicating a combined fleet total would overcount service.

  • All 25 available car service aliases use billion passenger-km/year. The file contains 120 detailed four-wheeler ES series in passenger-km and no corresponding VKM series. The mapping-file comment claiming vehicle-km is not evidence of the input unit.

  • All 20 declared REMIND truck mappings have exact service/energy labels. However, ten BEV/FCEV pairs have a zero 2020 numerator or denominator in EUR; a direct 2050/2020 intensity ratio is undefined. Existing Premise processing backfills missing intensities from later years before normalization. The resulting index is not an observed 2020 intensity.

  • The remaining 18 truck mappings declare no REMIND signal. This includes proxy technologies and hybrids; absence must remain visible.

get_iam_efficiencies currently substitutes ones when required aliases are missing. It backfills/fills missing ratios, normalizes to 2020, applies its monotonicity policy, and limits the vehicle efficiency index to 1.25. find_iam_efficiency_change also substitutes one for a nonfinite result. Consequently a factor of one does not distinguish no projected change from missing input. A raw ratio in the audit CSV is not necessarily the factor eventually applied by Premise.

Passenger-service intensity can reflect occupancy as well as vehicle energy use. Applying its relative change to a fixed-occupancy vehicle is an explicit transfer assumption, not a reconstruction of vehicle-km intensity. REMIND's public reporting source distinguishes ES in passenger-km and VKM after division by load factor; its FE names use energy carriers. See REMIND reporting source. That public source is corroborating methodology, not proof of the exact software revision that generated the local scenario.

Native-model sizing probes

The probe uses matching local 2020 carculator sources, Swiss climate, the Regional delivery truck cycle and the approved NMC-622 cell density of 0.26 kWh/kg for 2050. It first sizes a candidate pack from the existing IAM energy factor, then lets the native model reconverge mass and power without forcing its energy trace to match that factor. All runs retain the baseline range objective, duty, load and lifetime; reported ranges below are the actual capacity/energy result, not a claim that the objective was met.

The existing IAM factor is 0.8. With unchanged charging efficiency it targets 3172.98 kJ/km. Merely setting nominal capacity to 0.8 times the baseline does not reproduce that energy demand in the physical model. Applying all native future engineering assumptions would overshoot the IAM improvement. The physical model must be calibrated once against the intended final energy boundary, with bounded convergence and no second independent IAM multiplier. If charging efficiency changes, grid and stored-energy targets must be reconciled explicitly before sizing nominal capacity.

The Large BEV car currently receives a factor of one because its requested aliases are missing. Its 2020 pack is 97.44 kWh and baseline range 466.17 km. The approved density alone raises physical range to 471.65 km with unchanged capacity; native future hardware assumptions raise it to 537.41 km in this diagnostic. These are not approved final future inventories.

Pending scientific choices

Two specific questions were sent to the user:

  1. Where a technology-specific IAM signal cannot be recovered without an unsupported energy split, use explicitly documented native carculator technical projections, retain 2020 efficiency, or obtain additional IAM technology data. Correct unambiguous aliases first; a fallback must not conceal a repairable mapping error.

  2. For a valid IAM target, calibrate the extent of the native aerodynamic, rolling-resistance, lightweighting and drivetrain projections, or retain 2020 engineering with an explicitly lumped energy adjustment. The former can be bounded by the native physical assumptions and should expose infeasible targets rather than force a synthetic energy trace.

No dependent hardware changes have been installed while these choices remain pending. The existing PHEV regeneration-lifetime question also remains separate from the completed HEV buffer correction.
