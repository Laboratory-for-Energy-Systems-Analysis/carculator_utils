Passenger cars

The electric-mode PHEV battery was missing its distance-life input. Its zero default produced a nonfinite distance-based replacement calculation, which the legacy finite-value conversion reduced to zero. The combustion intermediate had the 200,000-km assumption, so the combined vehicle could also inherit a driving-share-weighted fraction of that intermediate's replacement count.

The user approved 200,000 total vehicle-km and the existing 18-year limit. The electric intermediate is now included in the seven existing distance-life parameter records; their values, uncertainty and source metadata are unchanged. One physical pack's replacement count is calculated as:

max(0, vehicle lifetime km / 200000 - 1,
       vehicle lifetime km / annual km / 18 - 1)

That count belongs to both driving modes before their costs are calculated and is retained when assembling the combined vehicle. It is not multiplied by electric driving share. At 200,000 km and 12,000 km/year, the 2020 car PHEVs still have zero replacements, so their inventory coefficients are unchanged. Comments and parameter/provenance companions now state the lifetime policy.

Regression cases independently check a 300,000-km vehicle driven 20,000 km/year (0.5 replacements) and a 72,000-km vehicle driven 2,000 km/year (1 replacement), at electric shares 0, 0.5 and 1. Both petrol and diesel PHEVs, replacement costs, labelled samples and caller-owned inputs are covered. All three parametrized cases failed before the correction and pass afterward.

The source commits are 1e97290 in carculator_utils and dc0fc4c in carculator; the truck arithmetic fix is e49c0d8 and the Premise inventories and integration regressions are 0be3f03. Matching sibling checkouts are needed for these changes together. Generation records capture the source hashes used before these commits were created.

Truck arithmetic defect

The inherited calculation assigned the electric intermediate the vehicle's entire lifetime travel, subtracted one initial pack, clipped the count, and then multiplied that replacement count by electric driving share during mode aggregation. Subtracting the initial pack and applying the three-replacement cap before usage weighting is mathematically different from applying those operations to actual lifetime battery use.

For a 40 t Regional-delivery PHEV, the current defaults are 710,000 lifetime km, 15% electric travel, a 60-km electric leg at 80% depth of discharge, nominal capacity 77.323265 kWh, and 3,000 equivalent full cycles per pack. Under the inherited net-energy approximation, lifetime electric travel is 106,500 km and uses 1,420 nominal-capacity cycles. A pack serves 225,000 electric km. The old 0.323333 replacement factor therefore becomes zero when the initial pack is subtracted once at the correct stage. At 50% electric travel, the same approximation yields 0.577778 replacements, rather than a usage-weighted count calculated before subtracting the initial pack.

The correction sets electric utility factor before replacement calculation, weights lifetime use before subtraction/clipping, and copies the one physical replacement count into both driving-mode costs. Nonzero explicit inputs remain whole-pack replacement counts. Conflicting nonzero values for the same physical pack are rejected. Zero continues to request automatic calculation. The existing fractional allocation, three-replacement cap and absence of a separate truck calendar-life model are retained.

Regenerative cycling is a separate unresolved boundary

Net stored-energy use subtracts recuperation. It consequently omits repeated charging and discharging during braking and acceleration, including use in combustion mode. That omission is material for the larger PHEV trucks.

dev/audit_phev_replacement_inputs.py adds the modelled reusable regenerative energy from both driving modes as a diagnostic, using the physical pack's discharge efficiency and weighting each mode by its travel share. It is a cycle-energy-balance sensitivity, not a calibrated battery-current trace or state-of-health prediction. The four large-truck cases checked separately use only 17--19% of positive combustion-mode wheel work as regenerative credit, so their existing positive-propulsion credit cap is not binding.

The detailed inputs are in truck_replacement_inputs.csv. These figures retain the model's existing cycle-life assumptions. The nominal-capacity EFC basis follows the cited experimental convention: cumulative discharged capacity is divided by nominal capacity. Depth of discharge is already used in the range/storage calculation and is not applied again as an extra divisor. Preger et al. (2020), Journal of The Electrochemical Society 167, 120532, defines this convention and evaluates chemistry and operating-condition effects (DOI, full text). The model's numerical 3,000-cycle default is retained; it is not a new fitted estimate or a claim of a universal measured PHEV pack life.
