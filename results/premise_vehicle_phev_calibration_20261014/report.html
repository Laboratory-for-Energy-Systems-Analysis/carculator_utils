Petrol and diesel PHEVs share these class-specific assumptions. Mini/Small use the Prius 8.8 kWh / 120 kg pack as an explicitly approved size proxy. Medium uses BMW 330e 12 kWh / 118.5 kg, excluding mounting brackets. Lower medium uses the Golf's 13 kWh nominal capacity; Large uses the 530e's 12 kWh; Medium SUV uses the RAV4's 18.1 kWh; and Van uses the Tourneo Custom's 13.6 kWh. Their inferred masses use the 330e pack-level density. Large SUV uses X5 24 kWh / 226.8 kg (500 lb), including mounting brackets. These different source mass boundaries remain explicit. The unavailable Micro PHEV inherits the Mini pack; no Micro PHEV is added to Premise's catalogue.

These are engineering and size proxies, not sales-weighted fleet averages or replicas of complete manufacturer vehicles. In particular, the Tourneo's series-hybrid architecture does not replace the model's powertrain. The RAV4 reference is a late-2020 vehicle. Other mode-specific components and control assumptions remain those of carculator.

Implementation and retained assumptions

Only 72 effective input cells change: 2020 PHEV pack mass and effective NMC-622 cell density. input_scope.json records the boundary. A retained 70% cell mass fraction determines the effective cell density needed to match both sourced nominal capacity and whole-pack mass. That fraction, the NMC-622 chemistry proxy, the electric-mode 80% usable-energy window and the inherited uncertainty bounds are modelling assumptions, not manufacturer measurements. The retained mass and density bounds are respectively +/-20% and +/-5%.

The electric-mode pack is copied into combustion operation during bounded mass sizing, including explicit capacity or chemistry overrides. Both modes therefore carry one physical battery. Conflicting overrides raise an error. The modes retain their own operating assumptions: combustion operation sustains charge, with zero net battery depletion. Copying the electric-mode discharge window as well would spuriously add battery range to combustion mode and under-purchase fuel. The regression suite caught that intermediate implementation; it was corrected before the final installation and builds. No hot/cold emissions coefficients were changed to compensate.

The 1.8 starts/day, 12,000 km/year, Swiss climate and existing electric utility-factor curve are retained. All calibrated packs have zero modelled lifetime replacements under the approved 200,000 vehicle-km / 18-year limits. The inventory still purchases nominal capacity times one plus replacements, independent of the electric driving share.

Fuel and electricity are recalculated, not merely edited in the workbook. For the Medium petrol PHEV, nominal capacity falls from 30.576 to 12 kWh, pack mass from 182 to 118.5 kg, electric range from about 155 to 62 km, and electric share from about 61% to 31%. Its fuel energy rises by 79.2%. Across the 16 PHEVs, fuel energy rises by 51.5--165.1% and purchased electricity falls by 32.7--61.2%. These changes follow the smaller electric range and existing utility-factor relationship, together with corrected mode masses. They must not be interpreted as a measured real-world fuel economy claim.

The regenerated workbook retains all 162 activity identities. Numerical changes are confined to the 16 PHEV operating datasets and their 16 vehicle production datasets. All other template coefficients are unchanged within relative tolerance 1e-12 and absolute tolerance 1e-18. Comments and structured metadata preserve model publications, source records, approved proxies and the existing HBEFA attribution.

Premise battery markets and future years

The complete car and truck traction packs use market for battery capacity (MIX scenario) in kWh. Both original kilogram-based lithium-ion manufacture and its separate disposal exchange are removed; the capacity market covers manufacture and end of life. The physical pack mass still controls vehicle sizing. Its mass and chemistry can differ from the MIX market's upstream manufacturing recipe, an explicitly retained boundary. The market's internal kilogram inputs are expected.

The existing bus workbook uses MIX and chemistry-specific LTO capacity markets, and the two-wheeler workbook uses MIX. Those workbooks have not been regenerated in this step. Trucks' separate lead-acid starter batteries remain kilogram-based; charging-station storage is separate from vehicle traction.

Future capacity uses the existing constant-range arithmetic: C = C0 * f, where f is the IAM-adjusted electric energy ratio before service-load normalization. For calibrated PHEVs, density follows the relative original- chemistry trend: d = d0 * d_NMC622(year) / d_NMC622(2020). Mass is M = M0 * f * d0 / d. NMC-622's 0.24 to 0.26 kWh/kg projection therefore improves each calibrated density by 8.33% by 2050, without resetting it to generic BEV density. This is a prospective modelling assumption. BEVs, trucks and metadata without the new relative-trend flag retain their existing projection behavior. No vehicle-model solve is added to Premise runtime.

In REMIND SSP2-NPi EUR, the Medium petrol PHEV changes from 12 kWh / 118.5 kg in 2020 to 9.6 kWh / 87.51 kg in 2050, preserving its modelled 62.09 km electric range. This combines a 20% electric-energy reduction with the relative density improvement.

Incremental EF 3.1 comparison

The controlled 2020 comparison covers 16 RER PHEVs plus six EUR PHEVs across 16 EF 3.1 headline categories: 352 paired scores per vehicle-kilometre, without weighting or normalization. RER forwarding activities can have the same score as their EUR operating supplier; the labels do not imply independent regional technologies. incremental_ef31.csv retains full method tuples and scores.

Climate impacts rise by 6.6--15.3%, while carcinogenic toxicity falls by 7.3--21.3%, non-carcinogenic toxicity by 10.6--25.4%, and metal/mineral resource impacts by 5.2--12.2%. The Medium petrol PHEV in EUR changes from 0.24253 to 0.26999 kg CO2-eq/km (+11.3%). Smaller batteries reduce manufacture and electricity use, while the lower electric driving share increases fuel use. The largest category increases are land use (+34.9%) and marine eutrophication (+26.7%) for the Large diesel PHEV; the largest reduction is ionising radiation (-43.9%) for the Large petrol PHEV in EUR.

dev/explain_premise_phev_calibration.py checks these four extreme cases with independent transposed-matrix solves and exact exchange-contribution closure (relative tolerance 2e-6). For the Large petrol PHEV, extra fossil tailpipe CO2 (+0.06597 kg CO2-eq/km) and petrol supply (+0.02176) exceed the reductions in electricity (-0.02976) and vehicle manufacture (-0.01840). The diesel supply increase dominates the Large diesel PHEV's land-use and marine-eutrophication changes. Reduced electricity supply dominates the ionising-radiation decrease. Detailed signed contributions are in incremental_ef31_contributions.csv. These large changes have identifiable inventory causes; no additional unexplained variation is identified in these four inspected cases. This does not validate the retained utility-factor curve or all underlying background datasets independently.
