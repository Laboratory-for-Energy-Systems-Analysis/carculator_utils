FCEV passenger-car battery calibration audit

14 October 2026. All eight installed 2020 FCEV car templates were rebuilt and checked. Their accounting is internally consistent, but the joint battery energy/mass/power calibration lacks a matched pack reference. Two manufacturer references support a materially heavier pack with less nominal energy at the same required buffer power. Adoption of a reference is awaiting the user's decision. No model defaults, installed inventories or scenario databases have changed by this audit. The user subsequently approved the scaled Mirai pack proxy. Its implementation and regenerated inventories are a separate step; the charging limit remains unsupported by a matched source.

Current implementation

The shared model sizes cells from the battery's share of net electrical power, then derives nominal energy and complete pack mass. The 2020 inputs combine 2.1304 kW/kg cell power density, 0.24 kWh/kg cell energy density and a 70% cell mass fraction. The power input cites several references together and an own assumption, without a matched pulse duration or SOC/temperature boundary. The resulting pack has 168 Wh/kg and 1.491 kW/kg.

All eight WLTC traces remain below the net stack rating when instantaneous fuel-cell response is assumed. This supplies no evidence about the buffer's actual peak capability, fuel-cell ramp response or startup support. The recorded battery power is a required allocation, not an independently verified rating. The inherited FCEV DoD of zero is a charge-sustaining range convention; it is not a physical zero usable SOC window.

Car energy calculations do not pass a battery charging limit to the shared energy model. Regeneration is limited by motor power and conversion/storage efficiencies. The new optional charging constraint used by trucks is available in utils, but does not automatically validate or constrain the car packs.

Manufacturer references

Toyota's 30 November 2020 Mirai announcement specifies 310.8 V, 4 Ah, a 44.6 kg pack and 31.5 kW output for ten seconds. Nominal energy calculated from voltage and charge is 1.2432 kWh. This is a late-2020 launch reference. Its paired pack quantities and declared pulse duration make it a useful candidate; using it across all car classes remains a proxy. The announcement provides no continuous discharge or separate charging envelope.

Hyundai's 2020 Australian Nexo emergency response guide (printed page 9, PDF page 12, visually checked) gives 240 V, 6.5 Ah, 1.56 kWh and 51.2 kg. Its 2018 launch specification gives 40 kW battery output without a duration. The guide and announcement list different motor ratings, 113 and 120 kW, and the latter explicitly qualifies market variants. This is a corroborating battery proxy, not a combined calibration of the complete vehicle.

These references do not prove that the existing generic pack is physically impossible. They provide a more traceable joint calibration and show why a capacity-only adjustment would be inappropriate. The retained 70% cell mass allocation and NMC-622 manufacturing identity would remain documented proxies; the proposed effective cell densities do not claim a measured internal layout.

Coupled model sensitivities

An offline CarModel subclass scales each reference pack by the required buffer power divided by the reference discharge rating. Energy and complete mass scale together inside the existing mass/power convergence loop. Motor rating, stack rating and hydrogen consumption consequently respond to the new mass. No reference is installed as a default. Fuel-cell power share, chemistry, efficiencies, usage and other vehicle assumptions are retained.

Charging evidence and separate diagnostics

No manufacturer charging envelope was established from the reviewed sources. The 2024 instrumented second-generation Mirai study shows that battery operation depends on driving cycle, SOC and temperature. Its measured operating traces do not establish a continuous charge-acceptance specification. First-generation Mirai tests concern a NiMH pack and cannot supply the missing rating for the new lithium-ion reference. A discharge rating is not reused as an asserted charging rating.

Two deliberately hypothetical charging caps, 50% and 100% of the modelled buffer discharge requirement, quantify sensitivity. They are not recommended limits or confidence bounds. Shared accounting supplies auxiliaries directly from the generator, applies the cap to the remaining DC power, and applies storage efficiencies only to the accepted battery energy. A third effectively unlimited case isolates the direct-auxiliary accounting difference.

For the Medium Mirai candidate, the 50% diagnostic cap is 20.24 kW: it curtails 2.97% of generator energy and raises hydrogen use to 0.90061 kg/100 km, 1.82% above the installed baseline. The 100% diagnostic cap does not curtail this car's WLTC regeneration; its 1.01% hydrogen increase includes the small benefit of direct auxiliary supply. Across all eight cases, the 50% diagnostic curtails 2.90--9.60% of generator energy and gives 1.59--2.29% total hydrogen increases. None of these limits have been adopted.

Premise inventory implications

All eight installed production datasets correctly purchase nominal kWh times one plus replacement count from market for battery capacity (MIX scenario). No parallel kilogram-based battery production or disposal exchange remains in those production recipes. All eight current replacement counts are zero.

The approved market policy preserves nominal capacity accounting rather than matching the physical mass of a dedicated high-power pack. Under the Mirai candidate, the Medium car's capacity purchase would fall from 4.46 to 1.60 kWh while physical pack mass rises from 26.6 to 57.3 kg. A lower battery LCIA burden would therefore reflect the retained capacity-market proxy; it would not demonstrate that the heavier physical pack requires less manufacturing material. This qualification was included in the user's pending calibration question.

If approved, implement the matched pack reference and metadata in carculator, then regenerate the eight FCEV car services and production templates. Retain the existing Premise policy of fixed 2020 FCEV hardware in future scenarios. The shared energy module already supports optional charge constraints; it requires a defensible car-specific limit before enabling that path. No new Premise runtime solver is required. Recheck fuel/carbon, capacity purchases, saved metadata and incremental EF results after an actual implementation.

Verification and reproducibility

dev/audit_car_fcev_buffer.py runs six cases over eight car sizes in the matched Python 3.12 / NumPy 1.26.4 environment. All 48 cases pass independent wheel/DC/fuel-energy and pack-mass/energy identities. The baseline reproduces the installed parameter companions and all eight capacity purchases. Workbook and parameter-file hashes are unchanged. The existing FCEV diagnostic and energy-physics suites pass 89 tests.

model_screen.csv records every case; pack_comparison.csv contains the three pack comparisons. installed_capacity_links.csv, reference_packs.json, source_review.json and run.json record inventory checks, evidence, source hashes and revisions. Downloaded source documents remain in the private workspace. No full background inventory is included. The broader final legacy-versus-refreshed EF comparison and the previously skipped PHEV cycling review are unaffected by this audit.
